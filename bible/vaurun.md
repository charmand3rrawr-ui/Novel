# Vaurun

The world he wakes on. Temperate, crowded, old, and comprehensively owned.

## The shape of it

One supercontinent and an archipelago chain, roughly nine hundred million
people. Cultivation and magic are both real, both ancient, and both organised —
Vaurun has had ten thousand years to decide who is allowed to be strong, and it
decided a long time ago.

**Essence is finite and local** (`LK-ESSENCE-FINITE`). Vaurun has been cultivated
in for a hundred centuries and it is *thin*. This one fact explains everything
political: the rich veins are all claimed, the old powers are defensive rather
than expansionist, and a newcomer who can generate essence from a source nobody
else can access is not a curiosity. He is an existential event.

## Who owns it

Four powers, each given one want, one method, one refusal, one weak point.

### The Ninefold Accord

- **Wants:** the ladder to stay exactly as it is.
- **Method:** licensing. Every technique above Kindling is registered; teaching
  an unregistered one is a capital matter.
- **Will not:** admit that the registry is a rationing system rather than a
  safety measure.
- **Weak point:** it depends on cultivators believing the ladder is fair.

### The Kiln Houses

- **Wants:** to remain the only reliable source of pills, blades and arrays.
- **Method:** the professions, and a monopoly on the materials for them.
- **Will not:** train an outsider past Artisan.
- **Weak point:** their failure rate. Every ruined batch sheds what he eats.

### The Verge Wardens

- **Wants:** to keep the thin places from collapsing.
- **Method:** measurement, quarantine, and quiet removal of anyone accelerating
  the drain.
- **Will not:** explain publicly how thin it has become.
- **Weak point:** they are right, and nobody believes them. They are also the
  only faction that identifies him correctly, and far too late.

### The Unlicensed

Not an organisation — everyone practising outside the registry. Tens of
millions of them. They are the novel's conscience: the people with the most to
gain from breaking the Accord, and the first population he uses as fuel.

## What he does to it

The novel must track this honestly, because the ending depends on the reader
having counted:

| Movement | What Vaurun loses |
|---|---|
| I | individuals — a handful, mostly people who attacked him |
| II | communities — a village, a sect, a Kiln house, a city district |
| III | regions, then the thin places, then the continent |
| IV | nothing. It is already gone. |

**The arithmetic is never offstage.** He was an actuary. He knows the number. By
the middle of Movement III the novel should be giving the reader the count, in
his voice, flat, because he cannot stop assessing and because the flatness is
the horror.

## What Vaurun is not

Not evil, not deserving, not a metaphor for the people who hurt him. The
temptation is to make the world guilty so the atrocity reads as justice. Refuse
it (`LK-VAURUN-INNOCENT`). Vaurun is a normal world with normal cruelties and
nine hundred million people who had nothing to do with a knife on a kitchen
floor in another universe.
