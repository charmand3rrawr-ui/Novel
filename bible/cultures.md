# Cultures

Three registers, because the novel needs three and no more. Each is defined by
how it handles obligation, which is how this world sorts people.

## Ledger-city (the chartered seaboard)

- **Belonging is:** being on a record that someone honours.
- **Greeting:** stating your house, or conspicuously not.
- **Rudeness:** asking what someone owes.
- **Generosity looks like:** a favour structured so it can be repaid, so the
  recipient is not humiliated.
- **Names:** given name plus family name, family name carrying the record.
  *Kestrel Vane, Sila Corr, Ifa Halloway.*

## Outer (station and moon populations)

- **Belonging is:** being counted in the life-support allocation.
- **Greeting:** naming your shift and your level.
- **Rudeness:** wasting anything measurable.
- **Generosity looks like:** giving someone your margin without telling them.
- **Names:** given name plus a family name carried from a dozen origins.
  *Zev Oduya, Nia Saal, Ammi Ventris.*

## Frontier (the Reach)

- **Belonging is:** having stayed a winter.
- **Greeting:** an offer of water, which is never refused and never billed.
- **Rudeness:** counting aloud.
- **Generosity looks like:** the default, which city people find unbearable.
- **Names:** plain given names, place-derived family names.
  *Wren Hollis, Coen Pike, Ode Alder.*

## Language notes

- No invented vocabulary except where the ledger requires it: *note, standing,
  pledge, discharge, burying entry, term, obligor.*
- Frontier speech drops the possessive where city speech insists on it. "The
  water" rather than "our water." Use this, do not explain it.
- Nobody in this world says "debt" casually. They say *standing*, and they say
  it the way we say *rent*.
