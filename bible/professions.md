# Professions

The second ladder, and the only one open to someone the talent assessors wrote
off. Professions turn effort into goods, goods into wealth, and wealth into
power — and the practice itself builds the practitioner.

## The nine ranks

Every profession uses the same ladder. Rank is certified by a guild and is
worth more than most titles.

| Rank | Name | Can make | Market reality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Apprentice | components, repairs | paid in food |
| 2 | Journeyman | common-grade goods | a living |
| 3 | Artisan | reliable refined-grade goods | comfortable; a shopfront |
| 4 | Master | profound-grade, with failures | wealthy; a waiting list |
| 5 | Grandmaster | profound-grade reliably, heavenly-grade rarely | a house courts you |
| 6 | Patriarch | heavenly-grade on purpose | a faction protects you |
| 7 | Saint | work that outlives dynasties | nations negotiate |
| 8 | Origin | invents; changes what the craft is | fewer than a dozen alive |
| 9 | Unwritten | disputed; possibly a story | — |

Advancing a rank requires a **certification piece** made under observation.
Failure is public and costs materials nobody at that rank can easily replace.

## The professions

### Blacksmithing — forging

Weapons, armour, tools, and the vessels other professions need. A smith's work
is the most legible power in the world: anyone can see a good blade.

- **Builds:** Strength, Constitution, Will.
- **Talent axis:** forging.
- **Signature risk:** a failed forging at Master rank and above releases what
  was being folded in. People die in forges.
- **Wealth:** steady and enormous. Every cultivator needs a vessel, and vessels
  break.

### Alchemy — pills, elixirs, poisons

The profession that most directly sells power: pills that raise realm, mend
meridians, awaken a stirred bloodline, or kill a Nexus in eleven seconds.

- **Builds:** Spirit, Comprehension, Perception.
- **Talent axis:** alchemy. **Kiln-Blood** bloodline is a cheat code here.
- **Signature risk:** toxicity accumulates in the alchemist. Old alchemists are
  rare and strange.
- **Wealth:** the highest margins and the worst enemies. A reliable
  realm-breakthrough pill makes its maker a target of every faction at once.

### Cooking — the quiet profession

Underestimated by everyone except the people who have eaten a Grandmaster's
work. Food carries essence into a body that would reject a pill, with no
toxicity and no rejection.

- **Builds:** Perception, Constitution, Fortune.
- **Talent axis:** cooking.
- **Why it matters:** alchemy forces power in; cooking *persuades* the body to
  accept it. Beyond Aperture, the body rejects most pills — and does not reject
  a meal. Every long-lived power keeps a cook, and nobody writes about them.
- **Wealth:** modest at low ranks, then abruptly beyond pricing. A Saint cook
  does not have a shop. They have a patron, or they have a nation.

### Inscription — formations and talismans

Fixing patterns so they hold without being driven. Wards, arrays, talismans,
and the formation floors that make a fortress a fortress.

- **Builds:** Comprehension, Will, Spirit.
- **Talent axis:** formation.
- **Signature risk:** a flawed array does not fail quietly; it does exactly what
  it was written to do.
- **Wealth:** contracted, institutional, and political. Inscribers know where
  everyone's defences are weak, which is a dangerous thing to know.

### Beast-taming

Contracts with essence-bearing creatures. Part husbandry, part negotiation,
part hostage-taking, depending on the tamer.

- **Builds:** Will, Perception, Spirit.
- **Talent axis:** taming.
- **Wealth:** volatile. One good contract is a fortune; most tamers die owing.

### Appraisal

Reading grade, provenance, and flaw. Neither glamorous nor optional — every
market, auction, guild, and treasury runs on appraisers, and a corrupt one can
move more wealth than an army.

- **Builds:** Perception, Comprehension.
- **Talent axis:** appraisal.
- **Wealth:** fees are small; the information is not.

## How professions feed power

Four channels, and the novel should use all four:

1. **Direct** — practising a craft raises attributes (the *Builds* line above).
   A smith who has folded steel for ten years is strong in a way training halls
   do not produce.
2. **Product** — you can use what you make. The best-equipped cultivator in a
   fight is usually the one who made the equipment.
3. **Wealth** — resources buy cultivation materials, instruction, and time,
   which is the scarcest of the three.
4. **Access** — rank opens doors that realm does not. A Grandmaster alchemist
   walks into rooms a Sovereign is kept out of, because the Sovereign needs
   something only they can make.

## The economics

- **Materials are the constraint,** not skill. Rank 4 and above consume
  materials that must be hunted, mined in dangerous places, or bought from
  someone who wants a favour.
- **Failure rates are the business.** A Master alchemist fails perhaps four
  batches in ten. The price of the successes carries the failures, and a run of
  bad luck ruins people.
- **Guilds set price and certify rank,** which makes them the real power in
  every chartered city — and gives them a stranglehold worth breaking.
- **Failed work still sheds essence.** This matters enormously to the
  protagonist; see `engine/system-engine.md`.

## Profession and realm

They are independent ladders. A Rank 5 Grandmaster smith at Kindling is common
and entirely respectable. It also means: professions are how someone who will
never be strong becomes someone who cannot be touched.
