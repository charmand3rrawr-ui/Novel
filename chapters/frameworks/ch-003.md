## Purpose

_Why this chapter exists. Test: what does the novel lose if it is cut?_

This is a **complication** beat inside *Entrance by the Side Door*, which promises: a person with nothing gets one impossible chance. The chapter's job in that promise is to someone sees something they should not have, and the chapter is about what they do with it.

- **Without it, the novel loses:** the outside view. Everything so far has been inside a man who believes he is invisible; this chapter proves he is not, and hands the knowledge to someone with her own reasons
- **It earns its place because:** it is the only place to establish that she has a novel of her own, before she does anything that would otherwise read as spite

## Goal

- **Plot goal** — what must be true at the end that was not true at the start: Sila has decided what Kestrel's anomaly is worth to her, and has chosen to say nothing — for her own reasons, none of which are mercy
- **Reader goal** — what the reader must feel, suspect, or fear by the last line: they should like her, and be frightened of what she is now holding

## Shape

**The Witness** — Someone sees something they should not have, and the chapter is about what they do with it.

- **Opening move:** POV may or may not be the one seeing. Consider giving this chapter to the observer.
  - _here:_ Sila's ordinary morning: the donor-board corridor, the plaque with her family's name, the letter from home about placing second
- **Turn:** The observer decides what it is worth.
  - _here:_ the flue report. She reads it, recognises the room, recognises the date — and the anomaly stops being about rank
- **Closing move:** Silence, sale, or leverage — and the protagonist does not learn which for chapters.
  - _here:_ she writes the report that would end him, reads it back, and puts it in her pocket instead of the box. The chapter ends without telling us why
- **This shape costs:** anonymity
- **Watch for:** The observer is a plot device. Give them their own agenda that has nothing to do with the protagonist.
- **Pairs well with:** demonstration, quiet-acquisition — consider for the next chapter.

## Failure modes

_Three specific ways THIS chapter goes wrong. Not generic craft advice._

1. her silence reads as attraction or as mercy. It is neither. Whatever her reason is, it should be legible as self-interest on a second read and unreadable on the first
2. the POV switch becomes a device for withholding. She must want something concrete in this chapter and either get it or not; the withheld reason is a bonus, not the structure
3. The observer is a plot device. Give them their own agenda that has nothing to do with the protagonist.
