# Kestrel Vane

`mc-kestrel-vane` · role: **protagonist** · culture: ledger · status: **active** (ch. 1) · seed: `authored`

> **Need that licenses them:** they are the novel.
> **Story function:** the finest reader of the books in their generation, asking whether anything can be given rather than owed.

## Core

- **Wound:** pledged at eleven to settle a family debt, and told it was an honour.
- **Lie:** worth is a balance you must keep positive, and love is the interest paid on it.
- **Want (conscious):** to become indispensable enough that no one can discard them again.
- **Need (unconscious):** to be known by someone who has the power to leave and stays anyway.
- **Fear:** owing something that cannot be repaid.
- **Moral axis:** loyalty vs justice — and beneath it, the few known vs the many unknown.
- **Truth that frees them:** a ledger cannot hold a person. Some things are given, not earned.

## Surface — what the reader meets first

- **Mask:** aggressive usefulness. Kestrel arrives having already done the work nobody asked for.
- **Verbal tic:** phrases everything as terms and conditions, including apologies and affection.
- **Physical signature:** ink permanently under the nails; hands always doing a second task.

## Capability

- **Spike:** reads financial instruments the way others read faces (`craft`, see `bible/the-ledger.md`).
- **Secondary:** knows who in any room actually decides (`insight`).
- **Hole:** cannot accept a gift without immediate repayment. This is the whole novel in one sentence.

## Bonds

- **Loyalty shape:** loyal to terms, not people. Will honour a bad deal to the letter and resent no one for it.
- **Leverage over them:** the pledge that bought their schooling is still an open entry, and it is not held by the house they think.
- **Leverage they hold:** they can read a burying entry, and almost nobody knows that yet.

## Offstage life

Paying down an obligation nobody knows about. Kestrel has been quietly
discharging their family's remaining standing, one instalment at a time, to a
family that has never asked and would not thank them.

## Trajectory

- **Arc shape:** corruption then recovery. Kestrel wins by becoming exactly what the house needs, and loses everything that made it worth winning.
- **Break point:** Arc 4 — the lie breaks in public, in front of the one person whose opinion they had priced as irrelevant.
- **Exit condition:** survives. Keeps the competence, drops the accounting, and makes one public, unaccounted gift.

## Progression

Tracks, gates, and the exchange rules live in `engine/progression.json`. The
short version: every point of capability is paid for with a point of intimacy,
integrity, anonymity, or body, and `lie_grip` falls only after an on-page
refusal, failure, or confrontation.

## Contradictions to play

1. The mask (relentless usefulness) cannot protect the spike: the better Kestrel reads the books, the more visible they become, and visibility is the one thing their wound cannot survive.
2. Their loyalty to terms rather than people guarantees they will one day be held to a term that costs them a person.
3. The lie — that worth is a balance — will look true for most of the novel, because in this world it very nearly is.

> "That isn't a favour, it's a standing with no term written down. Those are worse."

> "I don't need thanking. Thanking is a way of adding a line."
