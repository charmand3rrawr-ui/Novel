# Adam Rooke

`mc-adam-rooke` · role: **protagonist** · culture: earth-born · status: **active** (ch. 1) · seed: `authored`

> **Need that licenses him:** he is the novel.
> **Story function:** a man taught by three betrayals that attachment is a policy, handed a power that rewards him for treating the living as material.

## Core

- **Wound (age 9):** left at a service station with the bags, and he kept the bags neat for eleven hours because he thought it might matter to whether they came back.
- **Wound (age 33):** the company emptied over a weekend by three friends of fourteen years, while he was at the funeral of a father who had not spoken to him in twenty-four.
- **Wound (age 38):** the knife, and the eleven months between the policy and the use of it.
- **Lie:** every attachment is a policy someone has taken out on you, and the only safety is to be the one holding it.
- **Want (conscious):** to make existence pay what it owes him.
- **Need (unconscious):** to be wanted by someone who gains nothing by it.
- **Fear:** that he was always disposable, and the three betrayals were simply correct.
- **Moral axis:** the few known vs the many unknown — and he knows almost nobody.
- **Truth that frees him:** nothing owed him anything. That is not a cruelty; it is the condition of being alive, and he has been trying to invoice the weather.

## Surface — what the reader meets first

- **Mask:** courtesy. Genuinely pleasant, genuinely helpful, and it costs him nothing because none of it reaches him.
- **Verbal tic:** quotes probabilities at people. He assessed risk for a living and grief did not stop the habit.
- **Physical signature:** keeps his belongings unnaturally neat. He has done it since he was nine and has never once connected the two.

## Capability

- **Spike:** actuarial reading of any system — he sees what a thing is likely to cost before he sees what it is.
- **Secondary:** patience measured in months. The eleven months taught him what planning looks like, and he learned it from her.
- **Hole:** he cannot accept anything freely given. A gift reads to him as the opening of a policy, and he will find the premium even where there is none.

## Bonds

- **Loyalty shape:** loyal to no one, scrupulously fair to everyone. The fairness is worse.
- **Leverage over him:** anyone who is kind to him without wanting something. It does not soften him — it *frightens* him, and frightened is when he is most dangerous.
- **Leverage he holds:** nobody on Vaurun knows what he is. By the time the Verge Wardens identify him correctly, it is far too late.

## Offstage life

He keeps a list of nine names from the first life. He has never written it down and he recites it in order, and the order has never changed. Somewhere in Movement III the reader should notice he has stopped reciting it — not because he forgave them, but because the list stopped being large enough to matter.

## Trajectory

- **Arc shape:** tragic fall, visible to everyone but him, followed by a survival he did not plan for.
- **Break point:** Movement III, when he finishes and the feeling he was expecting does not arrive.
- **Exit condition:** he lives. He leaves. He takes the count with him.

## The count

He was an actuary. He knows the number of people he has killed, exactly, at every point in the novel, and he can produce it on demand. This is his defining trait and the novel's cruellest instrument (`LK-COUNT-STANDS`). It is never softened and never rounded.

## Contradictions to play

1. His courtesy is real and it is the thing that lets him get close enough to take. He is never crueller than when he is being kind.
2. The lie — that attachment is a policy — is the reason he survives Vaurun's first two movements. It works. That is what makes it hard to give up.
3. He is avenging himself on people who are not available. Every person on Vaurun is a substitute, and on some level he knows the substitution does not work, and he continues.

## Voice sample

> "You're assuming I want something. That's usually right. It isn't, here."

> "About one in nine. Better odds than most people get, so — yes. I'd take it."

> "I'm not angry with you. I want to be clear about that, because people find the other thing easier to bear."
